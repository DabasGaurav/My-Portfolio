<h2 id="problem">1. The problem: a sign-up isn't a commitment</h2>

Small NGOs run on volunteers, and they plan every session around a headcount. A coordinator in Jaipur put the problem in one line: about 30 people sign up for an event day, and about 18 come. Nobody tells her in advance, so she can't refill the gaps. Her fix is the one every NGO we spoke to uses: over-recruit, then chase people on WhatsApp the night before.

Volunteers aren't careless. They have limited, unpredictable time. When plans change, backing out of a commitment to a cause feels bad, so the easiest thing to do is say nothing.

**The result is a trust gap in both directions.** NGOs can't count on unknown volunteers, so they spend hours recruiting and chasing. Volunteers can't tell which NGOs are genuine, so many never start.

<h2 id="research">2. What we heard</h2>

Our team of six at ISB interviewed 10 NGO founders and coordinators, and 20 volunteers, across Delhi, Pune, Jaipur, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Shimla and rural Maharashtra.

The shared root cause: **there's no low-guilt way to signal a change of plan, and neither side has a record of the other.** Every match starts from zero.

That gave us our riskiest assumption, the one the whole product rests on:

<Callout label="Riskiest assumption">
  Volunteers who save a spot through Show-Up will either come, or free the spot early enough for someone else to take it.
</Callout>

<h2 id="bet">3. The bet: make backing out easy and early</h2>

The obvious fix for no-shows is penalties, and our own first spec had them. We went the other way. If saying "I can't make it" takes one tap and costs nothing a day ahead, people will say it, and the NGO gets the spot back in time to fill it.

**The biggest decision was cutting scope.** The first spec had trust levels, ID checks, standby lists, ratings and a test lab, built across two app modes. Looking at the first build, it was too much product for the problem. We rewrote it as a one-page build spec: one app, one database, every page real. Standby lists, group booking, certificates, payments, a mobile app and Hindi all went to "not in this version".

**Words mattered more than I expected.** The spec bans jargon: no "slots", no "release", no "reliability record". A volunteer saves a *spot* and *frees* it. Every time is a real day and time ("Free up your spot by Fri, 10 am"), never "48 hours before". Small, but it's the difference between a form and a promise.

<h2 id="ngos">5. The NGO side</h2>

For a coordinator, the whole product is one screen: **who's coming.** Posting is designed to take under five minutes and ends with a link to share. Before the day she sees a bar of who said yes, who hasn't replied and who can't make it, with the reason. Phone numbers appear only for people who have confirmed. After the day she taps ✓ or ✕ for each person, which builds every volunteer's track record.

<h2 id="build">6. How I built it</h2>

I wrote the spec and built the app end to end: Next.js and TypeScript on Vercel, with a Postgres database on Neon. A few things I'd point a reviewer to:

- **The rules live in one place.** When the check-in goes out, when a free counts as "early", how the track record adds up: all small, tested functions, so a product change is a one-line edit.
- **Timed emails are safe to run twice.** Every email has a unique key, so a job that runs late or twice never double-sends.
- **Launch-ready data from real interviews.** The live app opens with 17 activities from 10 interviewed NGOs, marked so they never get email and never count towards our metric.
- **An admin "view as".** Our team can open any NGO's dashboard exactly as they see it, which is how we walk an NGO through its own page.
- **Stress-tested sign-up.** A test that fired 25 sign-ups for one email at the same instant found 25 duplicate accounts. The database now allows exactly one. The same pass added limits on password guessing and closed a redirect hole.

<h2 id="metric">7. How we'll know it works</h2>

<h2 id="next">8. Where it stands, and what's next</h2>

Show-Up is live, but it hasn't run a real event yet, so the 80% is a target, not a result. What I'd do next, in order:

1. **Run the pilot** with the NGOs who asked to try it, and measure the number above.
2. **Get email delivered to everyone.** Reminders currently reach only our own address; a verified sender fixes that and unlocks email verification and password reset.
3. **Test the two-day timing.** It's a guess from interviews. If people still free spots late, the check-in should move earlier.

<h2 id="learnings">9. What I learned</h2>

- **Design for the honest exit.** The product isn't "save a spot". It's "tell us early when you can't come".
- **Cut until it's obvious.** The version people understood was a third of the first spec.
- **Plain words are a product decision.** "Free up your spot by Fri, 10 am" does more than any penalty rule we wrote.
